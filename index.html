Which video is better?

Each photo was taken with a long exposure, so things that moved are blurred. Two computer programs each turned the photo into a short video of what happened during the exposure. You see both videos, called A and B, without knowing which program made which.

  1. Watch both videos. They are short (five frames each) and play together in a loop; you can pause, step one frame at a time and change the speed.
  2. Answer two questions: which video is sharper and cleaner, and in which the motion looks more natural for this photo. Pick "No difference" if you cannot tell.
  3. Press Next. Your answers are saved after every pair, so you can stop and come back.

What to look for

The two examples below are drawings made for this tutorial, not videos from the study. They only show what each question means.

Question 1: which video is sharper and cleaner?

Look at single frames, mostly at the things that moved: pause and step frame by frame. Count leftover blur, doubled or ghost edges, smeared texture, flicker and invented patterns as "less clean". Ignore small differences in brightness or colour.

Question 2: in which video does the motion look more natural for this photo?

Let the video loop a few times, slow it down if needed. Natural motion is steady, follows the streaks you see in the photo and covers about as much distance as they show; things keep their shape, and what was still in the photo stays still. Either direction along a streak is fine: the photo does not tell which way things went.

How to judge

  • Judge the two questions separately. A video can be sharper but move less naturally, or the other way round.
  • Compare the two videos with each other, not with a perfect video. Neither needs to be good for one to be better.
  • Choose "No difference" only when you really cannot tell after stepping through the frames.
  • Which program is A or B changes from pair to pair, so judge every pair on its own.
  • Keys: space plays or pauses, the left and right arrow keys step one frame.
